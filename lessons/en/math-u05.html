Right-triangle trigonometry

What you will learn
  • Define sine, cosine and tangent as ratios of the sides of a right triangle.
  • Name the opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse for a given angle.
  • Find an unknown side or ratio, and know the special angle values.

Where do the ratios come from?

In a right triangle, once the acute angle θ is fixed, the ratio of any two sides is fixed however big or small the triangle is, because similar triangles have proportional sides.

So these ratios were given names: sine, cosine and tangent.

What are opposite and adjacent?

The hypotenuse is the longest side and it always faces the right angle. The opposite and adjacent sides change with the angle you are looking from.

The opposite side to θ is the one that does not touch it. The adjacent side touches θ and, together with the hypotenuse, forms it.

Special values

These angles appear again and again, so memorise them from the 30°−60°−90° and 45°−45°−90° triangles.

  • sin30°=21​, cos30°=23​​, tan30°=33​​
  • sin45°=22​​, cos45°=22​​, tan45°=1
  • sin60°=23​​, cos60°=21​, tan60°=3​
  • Notice that sin of an angle = cos of its complement, e.g. sin30°=cos60°.

Angle of elevation and depression

The angle of elevation is the angle between the horizontal and the line of sight to an object above you. The angle of depression is the angle between the horizontal and the line of sight to an object below you.

Draw the right triangle first: the vertical height is one leg, the horizontal distance is the other leg, and the line of sight is the hypotenuse. Because the two horizontal lines are parallel, the angle of elevation from one point equals the angle of depression from the other.

Laws and rules

Sinesinθ=hypotenuseopposite​Opposite and hypotenuse are lengths in the same unit; the result has no unit and is at most 1.
Cosinecosθ=hypotenuseadjacent​The adjacent side touches the angle and is not the hypotenuse.
Tangenttanθ=adjacentopposite​=cosθsinθ​The tangent can be greater than 1.

Worked examples

A right triangle has legs 3 and 4 and hypotenuse 5. Find sin, cos and tan of the angle θ opposite the side 3.
Show the solution
  1. The opposite side to θ is 3, the adjacent is 4, the hypotenuse is 5.
  2. sinθ=53​
  3. cosθ=54​
  4. tanθ=43​
  5. Check: cosθsinθ​=43​, which is the tangent.
Answer: sinθ=53​, cosθ=54​, tanθ=43​
A 10 m ladder leans against a vertical wall and makes an angle of 30° with the ground. How high does it reach?
Show the solution
  1. The ladder is the hypotenuse (10 m); the height is the side opposite the 30° angle.
  2. Sine links opposite and hypotenuse: sin30°=10h​
  3. sin30°=21​, so 21​=10h​
  4. h=10×21​=5 m.
Answer: The height is 5 m.
An acute angle θ in a right triangle has tanθ=43​. Find sinθcosθ.
Show the solution
  1. Draw a triangle: opposite 3, adjacent 4.
  2. Hypotenuse =32+42​=25​=5
  3. sinθ=53​ and cosθ=54​
  4. Product =53​×54​=2512​
Answer: 2512​
Common mistakes
  • Inverting the ratio: sine is opposite over hypotenuse, not hypotenuse over opposite. Remember sine and cosine are never more than 1 because the hypotenuse is the longest side.
  • Mixing up opposite and adjacent: always pick the angle first; the opposite side is the one that does not touch it.
  • Using tangent with the hypotenuse: tangent uses only the two legs (opposite and adjacent).
  • Mixing up the values at 30° and 60°: a smaller angle faces a shorter side, so sin30°=21​ is the smaller and sin60°=23​​ the larger.
Summary
  • sin = opposite ÷ hypotenuse, cos = adjacent ÷ hypotenuse, tan = opposite ÷ adjacent.
  • The hypotenuse is the longest side, so sin and cos never exceed 1.
  • Opposite and adjacent change with the angle; the right triangle solves height and distance problems.
  • Memorise the values at 30°, 45° and 60°.

Radians and the unit circle

★ Key
What you will learn
  • Convert easily between degrees and radians.
  • Compute arc length and sector area.
  • Find sin and cos of any angle using the unit circle, the reference angle and the sign.

The radian

An angle measures 1 radian if the arc it cuts off on a circle centred at its vertex equals the radius. The circumference is 2π radii, so a full turn is 2π radians.

Hence π radians =180°. To go from degrees to radians multiply by 180π​; from radians to degrees multiply by π180​.

Arc length and sector area

When the angle is in radians the formulas are very simple. If the angle is given in degrees, convert it to radians first.

The unit circle

A circle of radius 1 centred at the origin. If the radius turns through θ from the positive x-axis, anticlockwise, its end point is (cosθ,sinθ).

So cosine is the horizontal coordinate and sine is the vertical coordinate. Coordinates lie between −1 and 1, which is why sine and cosine do too.

  • At 0°, 90°, 180° and 270° the point lies on an axis: (1,0), (0,1), (−1,0) and (0,−1); for example sin90°=1 and cos180°=−1.
  • Two angles that differ by a whole number of turns (360° or 2π) end at the same point, so they have the same ratios; a negative angle is measured clockwise.

Reference angle and signs

For a large angle: find the quadrant, then the reference angle (the acute angle to the x-axis), take its value, and give it the sign of the quadrant.

  • Quadrant I (0 to 90°): sin, cos, tan all positive.
  • Quadrant II: only sin positive. Quadrant III: only tan positive. Quadrant IV: only cos positive.
  • Reference angle: in II it is 180°−θ, in III θ−180°, in IV 360°−θ.

Laws and rules

Conversionπ rad=180°degrees ×180π​ = radians; radians ×π180​ = degrees.
Arc lengths=rθs arc length, r radius (same unit), θ the central angle in radians.
Sector areaA=21​r2θr radius, θ in radians; area unit is squared.

Worked examples

Convert 120° to radians and 65π​ radians to degrees.
Show the solution
  1. 120°×180π​=180120π​=32π​
  2. 65π​×π180​=5×30=150
Answer: 32π​ rad, and 150°
A circle has radius 6 cm and a central angle of 3π​ radians. Find the arc length and the sector area.
Show the solution
  1. The angle is already in radians, so use s=rθ
  2. s=6×3π​=2π cm
  3. Area =21​×62×3π​
  4. =21​×36×3π​=6π cm²
Answer: Arc length 2π cm (about 6.28 cm), sector area 6π cm²
Find sin210° and cos210°.
Show the solution
  1. 210° lies between 180° and 270°: quadrant III.
  2. Reference angle =210°−180°=30°
  3. Values at 30°: sin=21​ and cos=23​​
  4. In quadrant III sin and cos are negative, so sin210°=−21​ and cos210°=−23​​
Answer: sin210°=−21​, cos210°=−23​​
Common mistakes
  • Using the arc formula with degrees: convert to radians first or the answer is wrong.
  • Forgetting that π in radians means 180°, not 3.14°.
  • Forgetting the sign after finding the reference angle: the value comes from the reference angle, the sign from the quadrant.
  • Swapping the coordinates: the point is (cos,sin), not the other way round.
Summary
  • π radians =180°, a full turn =2π.
  • s=rθ only when θ is in radians.
  • On the unit circle: the point =(cosθ,sinθ).
  • Reference angle, then the quadrant sign: I all, II sin, III tan, IV cos.

Circular functions: amplitude, period, range

★ Key
What you will learn
  • Recognise the graphs of sine, cosine and tangent.
  • Find the amplitude, period and range from an equation.
  • Write an equation from a given amplitude and period.

What are circular functions?

They are sin x, cos x and tan x, with x an angle in radians. They are called circular because their values are read off the unit circle, and periodic because the values repeat after a fixed length.

Sine and cosine have domain all real numbers, range [−1,1] and period 2π. Tangent has period π, vertical asymptotes, and range all real numbers.

The general form

The function y=asin(bx)+k (or cosine) changes the basic graph like this:

  • Amplitude =∣a∣: half the distance between the highest and lowest values.
  • Period =∣b∣2π​ for sine and cosine, and ∣b∣π​ for tangent.
  • Range =[k−∣a∣,k+∣a∣]: the number k slides the graph up or down.
  • A negative sign in front of a flips the graph over the x-axis but does not change the amplitude, which is always positive.
  • Horizontal shift: in y=asin(x−h) the graph moves h units to the right; the amplitude, period and range do not change.

Why is the period 2π ÷ b?

The function sin(bx) completes one full cycle when bx has travelled 2π. So x only needs to travel b2π​. A larger b squeezes the wave (smaller period).

Laws and rules

Amplitudeamplitude=∣a∣a is the coefficient in front of sin or cos; tangent has no amplitude.
Periodperiod=∣b∣2π​b is the coefficient of x inside sin or cos; for tangent the period is ∣b∣π​.
Range[k−∣a∣,k+∣a∣]k is the vertical shift (the constant term).

Worked examples

Find the amplitude, period and range of y=3sin2x.
Show the solution
  1. a=3, b=2, k=0
  2. Amplitude =3
  3. Period =22π​=π
  4. Range =[−3,3]
Answer: Amplitude 3, period π, range [−3,3]
Find the amplitude, period and range of y=−2cos2x​+1.
Show the solution
  1. a=−2, b=21​, k=1
  2. Amplitude =∣−2∣=2
  3. Period =1/22π​=4π
  4. Range =[1−2,1+2]=[−1,3]
  5. The negative sign flips the graph but does not change the range.
Answer: Amplitude 2, period 4π, range [−1,3]
Write the equation of a sine function with amplitude 5 and period 2π​ that oscillates about the line y=2, and find its range.
Show the solution
  1. Amplitude a=5 and k=2
  2. Period b2π​=2π​, so b=4
  3. Equation: y=5sin4x+2
  4. Range =[2−5,2+5]=[−3,7]
Answer: y=5sin4x+2, range [−3,7]
Common mistakes
  • Dividing only by b for the period: the correct value is b2π​ for sine and cosine (and bπ​ for tangent).
  • Calling the amplitude negative: amplitude is an absolute value, so −2sinx has amplitude 2.
  • Forgetting the vertical shift k when finding the range.
  • Saying the range of tangent is [−1,1]: tangent has no bounds.
Summary
  • Sine and cosine: range [−1,1], period 2π.
  • Tangent: period π, range all real numbers.
  • In y=asin(bx)+k: amplitude ∣a∣, period ∣b∣2π​.
  • Range =[k−∣a∣,k+∣a∣].

Identities, sum and difference of angles

★ Key
What you will learn
  • Use the basic identity and tan = sin ÷ cos.
  • Apply the sum and difference formulas.
  • Find exact values such as sin 75° by splitting the angle into two known angles.

The basic identity

On the unit circle the point (cosθ,sinθ) is 1 away from the origin, so Pythagoras gives cos2θ+sin2θ=1. It is true for every angle.

From it: sin2θ=1−cos2θ and cos2θ=1−sin2θ. This is the first tool when you know sin and want cos.

The second identity is tanθ=cosθsinθ​.

How to prove an identity

Start from one side (usually the more complicated one) and change it step by step into the other. Common tricks: write tan as sin ÷ cos, use a common denominator, replace 1 by sin2+cos2.

Sum and difference

They help with angles that are not special values but are a sum or difference of two special angles, such as 75°=45°+30° and 15°=45°−30°.

Watch out: sin(x + y) is never equal to sin x + sin y.

The formulas also work backwards: if you see sin50°cos10°+cos50°sin10° it is just sin(50°+10°)=sin60°.

Laws and rules

Basic identitysin2θ+cos2θ=1True for every angle θ, in degrees or radians.
Tangenttanθ=cosθsinθ​Defined when cosθ=0.
Sine of sum / differencesin(x±y)=sinxcosy±cosxsinyThe sign on the right is the same as the sign between the angles.
Cosine of sum / differencecos(x±y)=cosxcosy∓sinxsinyThe sign is opposite: cos(x + y) has a minus.
Tangent of sumtan(x+y)=1−tanxtanytanx+tany​For the difference, change both signs.

Worked examples

If sinθ=53​ and θ is acute, find cosθ and tanθ.
Show the solution
  1. cos2θ=1−sin2θ=1−259​=2516​
  2. θ is acute so cosine is positive: cosθ=54​
  3. tanθ=cosθsinθ​=4/53/5​=43​
Answer: cosθ=54​, tanθ=43​
Find the exact value of sin75°.
Show the solution
  1. 75°=45°+30°
  2. sin75°=sin45°cos30°+cos45°sin30°
  3. =22​​×23​​+22​​×21​
  4. =46​​+42​​=46​+2​​
  5. Numerical check: 42.449+1.414​≈0.966, close to sin75°.
Answer: 46​+2​​
Prove that 1+cosθsinθ​+sinθ1+cosθ​=sinθ2​
Show the solution
  1. Start from the left side and use the common denominator sinθ(1+cosθ)
  2. Numerator =sin2θ+(1+cosθ)2=sin2θ+1+2cosθ+cos2θ
  3. Replace sin2θ+cos2θ=1: numerator =2+2cosθ=2(1+cosθ)
  4. sinθ(1+cosθ)2(1+cosθ)​=sinθ2​ after cancelling the common factor (1+cosθ)
Answer: The left side turns into the right side, so the identity is true.
Common mistakes
  • Writing sin(x + y) = sin x + sin y: a function does not distribute over addition.
  • Forgetting the sign flip in cosine: cos(x + y) = cos x cos y − sin x sin y.
  • Ignoring the negative root when finding cos from sin: the quadrant of the angle decides the sign, so do not always take the positive root.
  • Reading sin2θ as sine of θ2; it means (sinθ)2.
Summary
  • sin2+cos2=1 and tan=cossin​.
  • To get cos from sin use the identity, then choose the sign from the quadrant.
  • sin(x ± y) keeps the sign; cos(x ± y) flips it.
  • Split a non-special angle into a sum or difference of special angles.

Double and half angle

What you will learn
  • Derive the double-angle formulas from the sum formulas.
  • Choose the right form of cos 2θ for the given information.
  • Compute exact values for half of a known angle.

Double angle

The double angle is a special case of the sum with two equal angles: sin2θ=sin(θ+θ). Applying the sum formula gives the laws below.

cos2θ has three equal forms. Choose by what you know: if you only know sin use 1−2sin2θ; if you only know cos use 2cos2θ−1.

Half angle

It comes from turning the cos2θ forms around: put 2θ​ in place of θ in them. Then sin2 and cos2 of the half angle are written with the cosine of the full angle.

When you take the square root, the sign comes from the quadrant of the half angle.

Laws and rules

Sine double anglesin2θ=2sinθcosθθ any angle.
Cosine double anglecos2θ=cos2θ−sin2θ=2cos2θ−1=1−2sin2θThree equal forms: pick the most convenient.
Tangent double angletan2θ=1−tan2θ2tanθ​Provided tan2θ=1.
Half anglesin22θ​=21−cosθ​, cos22θ​=21+cosθ​For the root, the sign comes from the quadrant of 2θ​.
Tangent half angletan2θ​=1+cosθsinθ​=sinθ1−cosθ​A form with no root, so no sign to choose; the denominator must not be zero.

Worked examples

If sinθ=53​ and θ is acute, find sin2θ and cos2θ.
Show the solution
  1. First cosθ=54​, because 2516​=1−259​ and the angle is acute.
  2. sin2θ=2×53​×54​=2524​
  3. cos2θ=1−2×259​=257​
  4. Check: (2524​)2+(257​)2=625576+49​=1
Answer: sin2θ=2524​, cos2θ=257​
Simplify 2cos215°−1.
Show the solution
  1. The form is 2cos2θ−1=cos2θ with θ=15°
  2. =cos30°=23​​
Answer: 23​​
Find the exact value of cos215°.
Show the solution
  1. Half angle: 15°=230°​
  2. cos215°=21+cos30°​
  3. =21+23​​​=42+3​​
  4. Check: 42+1.732​=0.933 and cos15°≈0.966, whose square is ≈0.933.
Answer: 42+3​​
Common mistakes
  • Writing sin2θ=2sinθ: you must also multiply by cosθ.
  • Saying cos2θ=2cosθ or cos2θ−1: only the three forms above are right.
  • Forgetting the division by 2 in the half-angle formula: sin22θ​=21−cosθ​, not just 1−cosθ. Also forgetting that the result is a square, so a root is needed to get sin2θ​.
  • Forgetting to fix the sign when taking the root for a half angle.
Summary
  • sin2θ=2sinθcosθ
  • cos2θ=cos2−sin2=2cos2−1=1−2sin2
  • sin22θ​=21−cosθ​ and cos22θ​=21+cosθ​
  • Pick the form of cos 2θ that matches what you are given.

Trigonometric equations and inverse functions

What you will learn
  • Solve a trig equation on a given interval and find all solutions.
  • Solve equations containing sin² or cos² by factoring.
  • Evaluate sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹ with their correct ranges.

Steps to solve a trig equation

1) Get the equation into the form sin x = number (or cos or tan). 2) Find the reference angle from the absolute value of the number. 3) Find the two quadrants where the function has that sign. 4) Write the solutions inside the required interval.

For example sin x equals 21​ in quadrants I and II, so the two solutions in [0,2π] are 6π​ and 65π​ (reference 6π​, then π−6π​).

For tangent: tanx=1 has two solutions in [0,2π], 4π​ and 45π​, because tangent is positive in quadrants I and III and its values repeat every π.

Quadratic-type equations

If sin² (or cos²) appears, treat sin x as a "variable" and factor like a quadratic. Then throw away any solutions outside [−1,1], since they are impossible.

If sin and cos both appear, use the basic identity to get one function only.

Inverse trig functions

sin−1y is the angle whose sine is y. Sine gives the same value for many angles, so the inverse is given one fixed range to make the answer unique.

That is why sin−1(sin150°) is not 150° but 30°: 150° lies outside the range of sin−1.

  • sin−1 has range [−2π​,2π​] and input between −1 and 1.
  • cos−1 has range [0,π] and input between −1 and 1.
  • tan−1 has range (−2π​,2π​) and any real input.

Laws and rules

Inverse sinesin−1y=θ⟺sinθ=y,−2π​≤θ≤2π​y between −1 and 1.
Inverse cosinecos−1y=θ⟺cosθ=y,0≤θ≤πy between −1 and 1.
Inverse tangenttan−1y=θ⟺tanθ=y,−2π​<θ<2π​y any real number.

Worked examples

Solve sinx=21​ for 0≤x≤2π.
Show the solution
  1. Sine is positive in quadrants I and II.
  2. Reference angle 6π​, since sin6π​=21​
  3. Quadrant I: x=6π​
  4. Quadrant II: x=π−6π​=65π​
Answer: x=6π​ or x=65π​
Find cos−1(−21​) and sin−1(−23​​).
Show the solution
  1. cos−1 has range [0,π]. Reference 3π​ and cosine is negative, so quadrant II: π−3π​=32π​
  2. sin−1 has range [−2π​,2π​]. Reference 3π​ and sine is negative, so the angle is negative: −3π​
  3. Check: cos32π​=−21​ and sin(−3π​)=−23​​
Answer: 32π​ and −3π​
Solve 2sin2x−sinx−1=0 for 0≤x≤2π.
Show the solution
  1. Let u=sinx: 2u2−u−1=0
  2. Factor: (2u+1)(u−1)=0
  3. So sinx=−21​ or sinx=1
  4. sinx=1: x=2π​
  5. sinx=−21​: sine is negative in quadrants III and IV, reference 6π​, so x=67π​ and x=611π​
Answer: x=2π​, 67π​, 611π​
Common mistakes
  • Giving only one solution: each sin or cos value has two solutions in one cycle (except 1 and −1, which have one), and tan has two in [0,2π].
  • Treating sin−1 as sin1​: the −1 here means inverse function, not reciprocal.
  • Giving cos−1 an angle in quadrant III or IV: its range is only 0 to π.
  • Dividing both sides by sin x and losing the solutions where sin x = 0: move terms and factor instead of dividing.
Summary
  • Reference angle from the absolute value, then the two quadrants matching the sign.
  • sin² or cos² means factor like a quadratic.
  • Ranges: sin−1 [−2π​,2π​], cos−1 [0,π], tan−1 (−2π​,2π​).
  • sin⁻¹ is not the reciprocal of sin.

Laws of sines and cosines, area of a triangle

What you will learn
  • Choose between the law of sines and the law of cosines from the given data.
  • Find sides and angles of a non-right triangle.
  • Find the area of a triangle from two sides and the included angle.

A triangle without a right angle

We name the sides a, b, c and the opposite angles A, B, C (side a faces angle A, and so on).

The right-triangle ratios are not enough here, so we use two laws that work in any triangle.

Which law when?

Law of sines: you have two angles and a side (a side and its opposite angle are known), and you want the other unknowns.

Law of cosines: you have two sides and the included angle and want the third side, or all three sides and want an angle. At a right angle it becomes Pythagoras because cos 90°=0.

Caution: if you are given two sides and a non-included angle, two different triangles may fit (an acute and an obtuse angle have the same sine), so check that the angles add up to 180°.

Area

If you know two sides and the included angle, the area is half the product of the two sides times the sine of the angle between them. It is the usual half base times height, since height = side times sine of the angle.

Laws and rules

Law of sinessinAa​=sinBb​=sinCc​Each side divided by the sine of the opposite angle; the three ratios are equal.
Law of cosinesa2=b2+c2−2bccosAA is the angle included between sides b and c, and a is the side facing it.
Area of a trianglearea=21​bcsinATwo sides and the included angle; area unit is squared.
Law of cosines for an anglecosA=2bcb2+c2−a2​When all three sides are known; a negative value means the angle is obtuse.

Worked examples

In a triangle A=30°, B=45° and side a=10 cm. Find side b.
Show the solution
  1. We know a side and its opposite angle, so use the law of sines: sinAa​=sinBb​
  2. sin30°10​=sin45°b​
  3. 1/210​=20, so b=20×22​​
  4. b=102​ cm
Answer: b=102​ cm (about 14.1 cm)
A triangle has sides b=5 and c=8 with included angle 60°. Find the third side a.
Show the solution
  1. Two sides and the included angle: use the law of cosines.
  2. a2=52+82−2×5×8×cos60°
  3. a2=25+64−80×21​=89−40=49
  4. a=7
Answer: a=7
A triangle has sides a=7, b=3 and c=5. Find its largest angle, then its area.
Show the solution
  1. The largest angle faces the longest side, which is a, so it is A.
  2. cosA=2×3×532+52−72​=309+25−49​=30−15​=−21​
  3. The cosine is negative, so the angle is obtuse: A=120°
  4. Area =21​×3×5×sin120°
  5. sin120°=23​​, so the area =215​×23​​=4153​​
Answer: A=120° and the area is 4153​​ square units
Common mistakes
  • Using the law of sines when no side with its opposite angle is known: start with the law of cosines instead.
  • Forgetting that the angle in the law of cosines is the one between the two sides and faces the first side.
  • Forgetting the minus sign in a2=b2+c2−2bccosA, or forgetting the final square root to get a.
  • Using a non-included angle in the area formula 21​bcsinA.
Summary
  • Sines: sinAa​=sinBb​=sinCc​.
  • Cosines: two sides and the included angle give the third side; three sides give an angle.
  • Area =21​bcsinA.
  • The law of cosines at 90° is Pythagoras.
