Linear Equations, Systems and Models

What you will learn
  • Solve a linear equation with inverse operations
  • Solve a system of two equations by substitution or elimination
  • Write a linear model from a word problem and use it

Solving a linear equation

A linear equation has the unknown x only to the first power. We want the value of x that makes both sides equal.

We isolate the unknown with inverse operations: adding cancels subtracting, dividing cancels multiplying. The golden rule: whatever you do to one side, do to the other so the equation stays true.

If there are brackets, expand them first, collect like terms on each side, then isolate.

If fractions appear, multiply both sides by the least common multiple of the denominators to clear them, then continue.

A system of two equations

A system of two linear equations has two unknowns and usually one solution: a pair (x,y) that satisfies both. Graphically it is the intersection point of two lines.

Substitution: write y in terms of x from one equation and put it in the other. Elimination: add or subtract the equations (after multiplying one if needed) so one unknown disappears.

After finding one unknown, substitute to get the other, then check in both equations.

A system may not have exactly one solution: if the lines are parallel (same slope) there is no intersection and no solution, and if one line lies on the other there are infinitely many solutions. Elimination shows it: a result such as 0=5 means no solution, and 0=0 means infinitely many.

The linear model

Many real situations grow by a fixed amount each step, so they are written y=mx+b. The number m is the rate of change (the increase per unit) and b is the starting value when x=0.

Example: a fixed membership fee plus a price per visit; the fee is b and the price per visit is m.

If two points are given, find m as the change in output divided by the change in input, then find b by substitution.

Laws and rules

Linear modely=mx+bm is the rate of change (slope), b is the starting value at x=0.
Rate of change from two pointsm=x2​−x1​y2​−y1​​Change in outputs divided by change in inputs.

Worked examples

Solve 3(x−2)+4=2x+5.
Show the solution
  1. Expand: 3x−6+4=2x+5, so 3x−2=2x+5.
  2. Subtract 2x from both sides: x−2=5.
  3. Add 2 to both sides: x=7.
  4. Check: left side 3×5+4=19, right side 14+5=19.
Answer: x=7
Solve the system: 2x+y=7 and x−y=2.
Show the solution
  1. Add the equations so y disappears: 3x=9.
  2. So x=3.
  3. Substitute in x−y=2: 3−y=2, so y=1.
  4. Check: 2×3+1=7 and 3−1=2.
Answer: (x,y)=(3,1)
Renting a hall costs 150 riyals for 3 hours and 310 riyals for 7 hours, and the relation is linear. What is the cost for 10 hours?
Show the solution
  1. Rate of change: m=7−3310−150​=4160​=40 riyals per hour.
  2. Find b from 150=40×3+b, so b=30.
  3. The model: y=40x+30.
  4. At x=10: y=400+30=430. Check: at 7 hours 280+30=310.
Answer: 430 riyals
Common mistakes
  • Moving a term across the equals sign without changing its sign: moving +7 gives −7.
  • Multiplying only the first term in a bracket: 3(x−2) is 3x−6, not 3x−2.
  • In elimination: forgetting to change the sign of every term when subtracting an equation.
  • Mixing up m and b in word problems: a fixed fee is b, not m.
Summary
  • Isolate the unknown with inverse operations, doing the same to both sides.
  • The solution of a system is the intersection of two lines; use substitution or elimination.
  • Linear model: y=mx+b with m the rate of change and b the starting value.
  • Always check by substituting into the original equation.

Solving Quadratics by Factoring

What you will learn
  • Write a quadratic equation in standard form
  • Factor a trinomial in several ways
  • Use the zero-product property to find the roots

The idea: a product equal to zero

A quadratic equation has standard form ax2+bx+c=0 where a=0. Before solving, move every term to one side so zero is left on the other.

Zero-product property: if u×v=0 then one factor is zero. So we factor the left side into two factors and set each equal to zero.

A solution is called a root; it makes the equation true and appears on the graph where the curve crosses the x-axis.

Ways to factor

Always start with the greatest common factor. Then pick the right method:

  • Trinomial x2+bx+c: find two numbers whose product is c and sum is b.
  • When a=1: multiply a×c, find two numbers with that product and sum b, split the middle term, then factor by grouping.
  • Difference of squares: x2−n2=(x−n)(x+n).
  • Perfect square: x2+2nx+n2=(x+n)2.

Warning: do not divide by the unknown

In 3x2=12x do not divide by x because you lose the root x=0. Move the terms and take x out as a common factor: 3x(x−4)=0, so the roots are 0 and 4.

Laws and rules

Zero-product propertyu×v=0⇒u=0 or v=0If the product of two factors is zero, at least one of them is zero.
Difference of squaresx2−n2=(x−n)(x+n)x and n are any two expressions; the result is the product of their sum and their difference.
Perfect squarex2±2nx+n2=(x±n)2The middle term is twice the product of the two terms, and the sign in the bracket is the sign of the middle term.

Worked examples

Solve x2−5x+6=0.
Show the solution
  1. Find two numbers with product 6 and sum −5: they are −2 and −3.
  2. (x−2)(x−3)=0.
  3. Either x−2=0 or x−3=0.
  4. Check: 22−10+6=0 and 32−15+6=0.
Answer: x=2 or x=3
Solve 2x2+7x+3=0.
Show the solution
  1. a×c=2×3=6; the numbers with product 6 and sum 7 are 1 and 6.
  2. Split the middle term: 2x2+x+6x+3=0.
  3. Factor by grouping: x(2x+1)+3(2x+1)=0, that is (2x+1)(x+3)=0.
  4. 2x+1=0 gives x=−21​, or x+3=0 gives x=−3.
  5. Check at −3: 18−21+3=0.
Answer: x=−21​ or x=−3
The square of a positive number plus three times the number equals 28. What is the number?
Show the solution
  1. Let the number be x: x2+3x=28.
  2. Move: x2+3x−28=0.
  3. Two numbers with product −28 and sum 3: 7 and −4. So (x+7)(x−4)=0.
  4. The roots are −7 and 4; the number is positive so we choose 4. Check: 16+12=28.
Answer: The number is 4
Common mistakes
  • Solving before one side is zero: (x−2)(x−3)=6 does not mean each factor equals 6.
  • Getting the signs of the two numbers wrong: to factor x2−5x+6 both numbers are negative because the sum is negative and the product positive.
  • Dividing by x and losing the root 0.
  • Giving only one root and forgetting the other, or ignoring a condition in the problem (such as "positive").
Summary
  • Make one side zero, then factor the other.
  • Set each factor equal to zero using the zero-product property.
  • Use the common factor first, then difference of squares or the trinomial method.
  • Check the roots in the original equation and in the problem conditions.

Quadratic Formula, Discriminant, Sum and Product of Roots

★ Key
What you will learn
  • Solve any quadratic with the quadratic formula
  • Decide the number of real roots from the discriminant
  • Find the sum and product of the roots without solving

The quadratic formula

Not every quadratic factors easily. For ax2+bx+c=0 we use the quadratic formula, which always works.

Where does it come from? From completing the square: divide by a and make the left side a perfect square to get (x+2ab​)2=4a2b2−4ac​, then take the square root of both sides, which is where the sign ± comes from.

Write the values of a, b and c with their signs first (careful: if b is negative then −b is positive), then substitute.

The discriminant tells the number of solutions

The expression under the root, b2−4ac, is the discriminant. We can know the type of roots without solving:

  • Positive: two different real roots (the curve crosses the x-axis twice).
  • Zero: one repeated real root (the curve touches the axis).
  • Negative: no real roots (the curve does not reach the axis); the roots are complex.
  • A positive perfect square (with integer coefficients): the roots are rational and the equation can be factored.

Sum and product of the roots

If the roots of ax2+bx+c=0 are r1​ and r2​ then (x−r1​)(x−r2​) expands to x2−(r1​+r2​)x+r1​r2​. Comparing with the equation divided by a gives the two laws below.

They help us find the second root when the first is known, find an unknown coefficient, and write an equation from its roots: x2−(sum)x+(product)=0.

A common use is the sum of the squares of the roots: r12​+r22​=(r1​+r2​)2−2r1​r2​.

Laws and rules

Quadratic formulax=2a−b±b2−4ac​​a is the coefficient of x2, b of x, c the constant.
DiscriminantΔ=b2−4acPositive: two real roots; zero: one root; negative: no real roots.
Sum of rootsr1​+r2​=−ab​r1​ and r2​ are the roots; a and b are the coefficients of x2 and x.
Product of rootsr1​×r2​=ac​c is the constant term and a is the coefficient of x2.

Worked examples

Solve 2x2+5x−3=0 with the quadratic formula.
Show the solution
  1. a=2, b=5, c=−3.
  2. Discriminant: 25−4×2×(−3)=25+24=49.
  3. x=4−5±7​.
  4. x=42​=21​ or x=4−12​=−3.
  5. Check at −3: 18−15−3=0.
Answer: x=21​ or x=−3
For which value of k does x2−6x+k=0 have exactly one real root? When does it have no real roots?
Show the solution
  1. Discriminant: 36−4k.
  2. One root means discriminant zero: 36−4k=0, so k=9.
  3. No real roots means a negative discriminant: 36−4k<0, that is k>9.
  4. Check: at k=9 the equation is (x−3)2=0 with the single root 3.
Answer: k=9; no real roots when k>9
One root of x2−5x+k=0 is 2. Find the other root and the value of k, then write an equation whose roots are 3 and −2.
Show the solution
  1. Sum =−1−5​=5, so the other root is 5−2=3.
  2. Product =k=2×3=6.
  3. For the last equation: sum 3+(−2)=1 and product 3×(−2)=−6.
  4. The equation: x2−x−6=0. Check: (x−3)(x+2)=x2−x−6.
Answer: Other root 3, k=6, and the equation x2−x−6=0
Common mistakes
  • Forgetting that the sum is −ab​, not ab​: the sign flips.
  • Substituting a negative b without brackets: b2 is always positive, for example (−5)2=25.
  • When simplifying: dividing only one term of the numerator by 2a; the denominator 2a divides the whole numerator.
  • Using the sum and product laws when a=1 without dividing by a.
Summary
  • Quadratic formula: x=2a−b±b2−4ac​​.
  • Discriminant positive: two roots, zero: one root, negative: no real roots.
  • Sum of roots −ab​ and product ac​.
  • Equation from its roots: x2−(sum)x+product=0.

Complex Numbers

What you will learn
  • Know the imaginary unit and the powers of i
  • Add and multiply complex numbers
  • Divide using the conjugate

The imaginary unit

No real number has a negative square, so we introduce the imaginary unit i with i2=−1. With it we can solve x2+4=0 and get x=±2i, and −9​=3i.

A complex number has the form a+bi where a is the real part and b is the imaginary part. We plot it on the complex plane: horizontal for real, vertical for imaginary. Two complex numbers are equal when their real parts are equal and their imaginary parts are equal.

Powers of i repeat every four

i1=i, then i2=−1, then i3=−i, then i4=1, and the cycle restarts. To find in divide n by 4 and use the remainder: remainder 0 gives 1, 1 gives i, 2 gives −1, 3 gives −i.

Operations and the conjugate

Add or subtract: combine the real parts together and the imaginary parts together. Multiply: expand like two polynomials, then replace i2 by −1.

The conjugate of a+bi is a−bi, and their product a2+b2 is a real number. To divide complex numbers, multiply the numerator and denominator by the conjugate of the denominator.

When the discriminant of a quadratic is negative, its roots are a pair of complex conjugates.

Laws and rules

Imaginary uniti2=−1So i3=−i and i4=1.
Number times its conjugate(a+bi)(a−bi)=a2+b2Always a real number.

Worked examples

Find i27.
Show the solution
  1. Divide 27 by 4: quotient 6, remainder 3.
  2. i27=i3=−i.
  3. Check: i24=1 so i27=1×i3=−i.
Answer: −i
Find (3+2i)(1+i).
Show the solution
  1. Expand: 3×1+3×i+2i×1+2i×i.
  2. =3+3i+2i+2i2.
  3. Replace i2=−1: 3+5i−2.
  4. =1+5i.
Answer: 1+5i
Write 1−i7+i​ in the form a+bi.
Show the solution
  1. The conjugate of the denominator 1−i is 1+i; multiply the numerator and denominator by it.
  2. Numerator: (7+i)(1+i)=7+7i+i+i2=6+8i.
  3. Denominator: (1−i)(1+i)=1+1=2.
  4. 26+8i​=3+4i. Check: (3+4i)(1−i)=3−3i+4i+4=7+i.
Answer: 3+4i
Common mistakes
  • Believing i2=1; the correct value is i2=−1.
  • Leaving i2 in the result after multiplying instead of replacing it with −1.
  • Dividing a complex number by dividing each part by the denominator without using the conjugate.
  • When computing in: using the quotient instead of the remainder.
Summary
  • i2=−1 and the powers of i repeat every 4.
  • A complex number a+bi has a real part and an imaginary part.
  • To multiply, expand then replace i2=−1.
  • To divide, multiply by the conjugate of the denominator.

Operations on Polynomials and Polynomial Functions

★ Key
What you will learn
  • Add, subtract and multiply polynomials
  • Evaluate a polynomial function
  • Read the degree and the roots from a graph

What is a polynomial?

A polynomial is a sum of terms, each a number times a non-negative whole power of the variable, such as 2x3−x+5. Its degree is the highest power (here 3) and the leading coefficient is the coefficient of that power (here 2).

We write it in standard form: powers from largest to smallest. A polynomial of degree 1 is called linear, of degree 2 quadratic, and of degree 3 cubic.

Adding, subtracting and multiplying

Add and subtract: combine only like terms, those with the same power. When subtracting, change the sign of every term of the polynomial being subtracted.

Multiply: multiply every term of the first by every term of the second, and add the exponents when multiplying powers: x2×x3=x5. The degree of the product is the sum of the degrees.

Famous products: (x+n)2=x2+2nx+n2 and (x−n)(x+n)=x2−n2.

Polynomial functions

To find f(k) put k in place of every x and compute. For an expression such as f(x+1) put the whole bracket in place of x.

The roots (zeros) are the values of x where f(x)=0, the intersections with the x-axis. The number of real roots is never more than the degree.

The leading term controls the ends of the graph: for an odd degree the ends go opposite ways, for an even degree they go the same way. If the leading coefficient is positive, the right end of the graph rises.

Laws and rules

Multiplying powersxm×xn=xm+nAdd the exponents when multiplying.
Square of a sum(x+n)2=x2+2nx+n2Do not forget the middle term 2nx.

Worked examples

Find (2x2+3x−5)−(x2−4x+2).
Show the solution
  1. Change the sign of every term in the second bracket: 2x2+3x−5−x2+4x−2.
  2. x2 terms: 2−1=1, giving x2.
  3. x terms: 3+4=7, giving 7x. Constants: −5−2=−7.
Answer: x2+7x−7
Find (x+3)(2x2−x+4).
Show the solution
  1. Multiply x by each term: 2x3−x2+4x.
  2. Multiply 3 by each term: 6x2−3x+12.
  3. Combine like terms: 2x3+(−1+6)x2+(4−3)x+12.
  4. Check at x=1: 4×5=20 and 2+5+1+12=20.
Answer: 2x3+5x2+x+12
If f(x)=2x2−x+3 find f(2) and f(x+1)−f(x).
Show the solution
  1. f(2)=2×4−2+3=9.
  2. f(x+1)=2(x+1)2−(x+1)+3=2x2+4x+2−x−1+3=2x2+3x+4.
  3. Subtract f(x): (2x2+3x+4)−(2x2−x+3)=4x+1.
  4. Check at x=0: f(1)−f(0)=4−3=1 and 4×0+1=1.
Answer: f(2)=9 and f(x+1)−f(x)=4x+1
Common mistakes
  • When subtracting: changing the sign of only the first term of the bracket.
  • Adding unlike terms, such as 2x2 with 3x.
  • Writing (x+n)2=x2+n2 and forgetting the middle term.
  • When multiplying powers: multiplying the exponents instead of adding them.
Summary
  • The degree is the highest power; the leading term shapes the ends of the graph.
  • Add and subtract only like terms; multiply by distributing and adding exponents.
  • To evaluate a function, replace every x with the whole bracket.
  • Roots are where the curve meets the x-axis, and there are never more than the degree.

Division, Remainder and Factor Theorems, Solving Polynomial Equations

★ Key
What you will learn
  • Divide polynomials by long and synthetic division
  • Use the Remainder and Factor theorems
  • Solve a cubic by finding one root and then factoring

Dividing polynomials

It is like long division of numbers: divide the first term of the dividend by the first term of the divisor, multiply the result by the divisor, subtract, bring down the next term and repeat until the remainder has lower degree than the divisor.

The result is written: dividend = divisor × quotient + remainder.

When the divisor is (x−k) we use synthetic division: write the coefficients of the dividend (with zeros for missing powers), bring down the first one, then multiply by k and add column by column.

Remainder and Factor theorems

Remainder Theorem: the remainder when f(x) is divided by (x−k) equals f(k). So we do not need division to know the remainder; substituting is enough. For the divisor (x+2) substitute k=−2, and for (2x−1) substitute k=21​.

Factor Theorem: (x−k) is a factor of f(x) if and only if f(k)=0, that is the remainder is zero and k is a root.

Solving a cubic equation

Steps: (1) find a root by trial among the divisors of the constant term (such as ±1, ±2, ±3, ±6 when the constant is 6). (2) When substitution gives zero, divide synthetically by (x−k). (3) Factor the quadratic quotient. (4) Set each factor equal to zero.

Sometimes you can factor by grouping directly. The number of roots is never more than the degree.

If the leading coefficient is not 1, also try fractions (a divisor of the constant) over (a divisor of the leading coefficient), such as ±21​ and ±23​.

Laws and rules

Division algorithmf(x)=(x−k)×q(x)+Rq(x) is the quotient and R the remainder (a constant when dividing by x−k).
Remainder TheoremR=f(k)The remainder when f(x) is divided by (x−k).
Factor Theoremf(k)=0⟺(x−k) is a factorf is a polynomial and k is a real number.

Worked examples

Divide x2+5x+7 by (x+2).
Show the solution
  1. x2÷x=x; multiply x(x+2)=x2+2x and subtract: 3x+7 remains.
  2. 3x÷x=3; multiply 3(x+2)=3x+6 and subtract: 1 remains.
  3. The quotient is x+3 and the remainder is 1 (its degree is less than the divisor).
  4. Check: (x+2)(x+3)+1=x2+5x+6+1=x2+5x+7.
Answer: Quotient x+3, remainder 1
(a) What is the remainder when x3−2x2+4 is divided by (x+1)? (b) For which k is (x−1) a factor of x3+kx2−x−2?
Show the solution
  1. (a) Substitute x=−1: −1−2+4=1.
  2. (b) (x−1) is a factor means f(1)=0.
  3. 1+k−1−2=0 so k−2=0.
  4. So k=2. Check: 1+2−1−2=0.
Answer: (a) remainder 1, (b) k=2
Solve x3−2x2−5x+6=0.
Show the solution
  1. Try divisors of 6: f(1)=1−2−5+6=0, so (x−1) is a factor.
  2. Synthetic division by 1 gives the coefficients 1,−1,−6 and remainder 0 (see the figure).
  3. The quotient x2−x−6=(x−3)(x+2).
  4. The equation: (x−1)(x−3)(x+2)=0.
  5. Check: the product of the roots is 1×3×(−2)=−6, which is −16​ (the constant over the leading coefficient, with the sign reversed).
Answer: x=1 or x=3 or x=−2
Common mistakes
  • Substituting k with the same sign when dividing by (x+2); you must substitute −2.
  • Forgetting a zero for a missing power in synthetic division: x3+2x−1 needs the coefficients 1,0,2,−1.
  • Thinking (x−k) is a factor when the remainder is not zero; a factor needs remainder zero.
  • Stopping at one root of a cubic and ignoring the two roots of the quadratic quotient.
Summary
  • Dividend = divisor × quotient + remainder.
  • The remainder on dividing by (x−k) is f(k).
  • (x−k) is a factor if and only if f(k)=0.
  • To solve a cubic: find a root by trial, divide synthetically, factor the quotient.

Percentages, Ratios, Proportion and Rates

What you will learn
  • Compute percentages, discounts and increases
  • Compute percent change and understand successive changes
  • Solve ratio, proportion and rate problems

Percentages

A percentage is a part of 100: 25% means 10025​. To take a percentage of a quantity multiply the quantity by the matching fraction. To find "what percent", divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100.

A discount is subtracted from the original price and an increase is added to it. You can also multiply directly: after a 30% discount, 70% of the price remains.

To find the whole from a part: whole = part ÷ (percent ÷100). For example, if 20% of a number is 30, the number is 30÷0.2=150.

Percent change

Percent change = (new value − old value) divided by the old value, then ×100. The divisor is always the old (original) value.

That is why an increase and a decrease by the same amount are not the same percent: rising from 100 to 120 is 20%, but going back from 120 to 100 is a fall of about 16.7%. A 20% increase then a 20% decrease gives 1.2×0.8=0.96, a 4% fall from the original.

Ratio, proportion and rate

The ratio a:b compares two quantities of the same kind. To share a quantity in a ratio add the parts, find the value of one part, then multiply by the number of parts for each side.

A proportion says two ratios are equal: ba​=dc​, solved by cross multiplication. A rate compares two quantities with different units, such as km per hour, and the unit rate is the quotient.

In inverse proportion the product of the two quantities stays constant: more workers means fewer days, so workers × days is constant.

Laws and rules

Part of a wholepart=100percent​×wholeThe percent is a number out of 100.
Percent changeoldnew−old​×100Positive means an increase, negative a decrease.
Proportionba​=dc​⇒a×d=b×cCross multiplication.

Worked examples

A pair of shoes costs 250 riyals with a 30% discount. What is the new price?
Show the solution
  1. The discount: 10030​×250=75 riyals.
  2. New price: 250−75=175 riyals.
  3. Check the second way: 70% of the price remains, 0.7×250=175.
Answer: 175 riyals
A price rose from 80 riyals to 100 riyals. What is the percent increase? What is the percent decrease if it goes back from 100 to 80?
Show the solution
  1. The difference is 100−80=20.
  2. Increase: 8020​×100=25% (divide by the old value 80).
  3. Decrease: the difference is 20 and the old value is now 100, so 10020​×100=20%.
  4. Check: 80×1.25=100 and 100×0.8=80.
Answer: a 25% increase, then a 20% decrease
A painter mixes two colours in the ratio 3:5 and needs 24 litres of mixture. How many litres of each colour?
Show the solution
  1. Number of parts: 3+5=8.
  2. Value of one part: 24÷8=3 litres.
  3. First colour: 3×3=9 litres, second: 5×3=15 litres.
  4. Check: 9+15=24 and 9:15=3:5.
Answer: 9 litres and 15 litres
Common mistakes
  • Computing percent change by dividing by the new value instead of the old one.
  • Treating a successive increase and decrease as one percent: +20% then −20% does not return to the original price (the result is 96% of it).
  • Dividing the quantity by one number of the ratio instead of their sum when sharing a quantity.
  • Comparing two rates with different units without converting.
Summary
  • 25% means 10025​; a discount is subtracted and an increase is added.
  • Percent change is always divided by the old value.
  • To share in a ratio: add the parts, find one part, then multiply.
  • A proportion is solved by cross multiplication; a rate compares two different quantities.
