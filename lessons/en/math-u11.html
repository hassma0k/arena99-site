Ratio and Proportion

★ Key
What you will learn
  • Write and simplify a ratio of two quantities
  • Solve direct proportion by cross-multiplying or by the unit value
  • Share a quantity in a given ratio
  • Tell direct proportion from inverse proportion

What is a ratio?

A ratio compares two quantities of the same kind. It is written 3:5 or 53​ and read «3 to 5». Order matters: the ratio of boys to girls is not the ratio of girls to boys.

Simplify a ratio by dividing both terms by the same number, so 45:60 becomes 3:4. If the units differ, make them the same first: 30 minutes to 2 hours is 30:120=1:4.

Ratios are kept by multiplying, not adding

If the ratio is 3:5, every 3 boys go with 5 girls. If the boys become 8 times as many, the girls become 8 times as many too, so each part of the ratio stands for 8 students.

Adding the same number to both terms changes the ratio. Multiplying or dividing both by one number keeps it.

Direct proportion

A proportion says two ratios are equal: yx​=wz​. Solve it by cross-multiplying: x×w=y×z.

In direct proportion, when one quantity grows the other grows by the same factor, and their quotient stays constant (like the price of one pen). The fastest method: find the unit value, then multiply.

Sharing a quantity in a ratio

Add the parts of the ratio, divide the quantity by that total to get the value of one part, then multiply by the number of parts each side has.

Example: a class of 30 students in the ratio 3:2. The total is 5 parts, one part is 30÷5=6, so there are 18 boys and 12 girls.

Inverse proportion

In inverse proportion, when one quantity grows the other shrinks, and their product stays constant: more workers need fewer days to do the same job.

Fix the product (the total amount of work), then divide it by the new quantity.

Laws and rules

Simplifying a ratiox:y=(x÷n):(y÷n)x and y are the terms of the ratio and n divides both (usually the greatest common factor).
Proportionyx​=wz​Two equal ratios.
Cross-multiplicationx×w=y×zThe product of the extremes equals the product of the means.
Value of one partpart=sum of the partsquantity​Multiply by the number of parts each side has to get its share.
Inverse proportionx×y=constantExample: workers × days = total work, which is constant.

Worked examples

5 pens cost 15 SAR. How much do 8 pens cost at the same price?
Show the solution
  1. Direct proportion: more pens cost proportionally more.
  2. Price of one pen =15÷5=3 SAR.
  3. Cost of 8 pens =8×3=24 SAR.
  4. Check: 515​=824​=3.
Answer: 24 SAR
A sum of 360 SAR is shared among Ahmad, Khalid and Salem in the ratio 2:3:4. How much does each get?
Show the solution
  1. Sum of the parts =2+3+4=9.
  2. One part =360÷9=40 SAR.
  3. Ahmad =2×40=80, Khalid =3×40=120, Salem =4×40=160.
  4. Check: 80+120+160=360.
Answer: Ahmad 80, Khalid 120, Salem 160 SAR
4 workers finish a job in 15 days. How many days do 6 workers need at the same rate?
Show the solution
  1. More workers, fewer days: inverse proportion, so the product is constant.
  2. Total work =4×15=60 worker-days.
  3. Days =60÷6=10.
  4. Check: 6 workers is more than 4, so fewer than 15 days, and 10<15.
Answer: 10 days
Common mistakes
  • Adding a number to both terms: to go from 3:5 to a ratio with 24 boys, adding 21 to both is wrong. Multiply: 24÷3=8, then 5×8=40.
  • Reversing the ratio: «boys to girls» means the first number belongs to the boys. Label each term before solving.
  • Dividing the quantity by one term instead of the sum of the parts: in 3:5 the total is 8, not 3 or 5.
  • Using cross-multiplication on an inverse problem: workers and days are multiplied to give a constant, not divided.
Summary
  • A ratio is kept by multiplying or dividing both terms by one number.
  • Direct proportion: find the unit value or cross-multiply.
  • To share a quantity: add the parts, divide, then multiply.
  • Inverse proportion: the product stays constant.

Percentages

★ Key
What you will learn
  • Convert a percent to a fraction and a decimal
  • Find a percent of a quantity
  • Find what percent a part is of a whole
  • Compute a percent increase or decrease on the original value

What a percent means

A percent is a fraction over 100, so 35%=10035​=0.35. Convert the % sign into a fraction or decimal before calculating.

Memorise: 50%=21​, 25%=41​, 20%=51​, 10%=101​.

Finding a percent of a quantity

The word «of» means multiply: 20% of 150 is 10020​×150=30.

Quick trick: 10% of a number is the number divided by 10, then double or halve. 10% of 150 is 15, so 20% is 30.

Finding the percent

To see what percent one number is of another, divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100: 45 out of 60 is 6045​×100=75%.

To turn any fraction into a percent, multiply it by 100: 83​×100=37.5%.

A ratio 3:2 means the boys are 3 out of every 5 parts, which is 53​=60% of the total.

Percent increase and decrease

A percent change is always measured on the original value before the change, not the new one. After a 15% rise the price is 115% of the original (multiply by 1.15); after a 15% fall it is 85% (multiply by 0.85).

So a price going from 80 to 100 rises 25%, but going from 100 down to 80 it falls only 20%.

Laws and rules

Part of a quantitypart=100percent​×wholeThe percent is the number (such as 20); the whole is the original quantity.
The percentpercent=wholepart​×100%Part and whole in the same unit.
Percent changepercent change=originalchange​×100%The change is the difference between the new and the original value.
New valuenew=original×(1±100percent​)Use + for an increase and − for a decrease.

Worked examples

What is 20% of 150?
Show the solution
  1. «Of» means multiply, so turn 20% into a fraction: 10020​=51​.
  2. Value =150×51​=30.
  3. Check: 10% of 150 is 15, and double that is 30.
Answer: 30
The price of an item rises from 200 SAR by 15%. What is the new price?
Show the solution
  1. Increase =10015​×200=30 SAR.
  2. New price =200+30=230 SAR.
  3. Check another way: 200×1.15=230.
Answer: 230 SAR
A price rises from 80 SAR to 100 SAR. What is the percent increase? If it fell from 100 to 80, what would the percent decrease be?
Show the solution
  1. Increase =100−80=20 SAR, measured on the original 80: 8020​=41​=25%.
  2. In the second case the drop is also 20, but the original is now 100: 10020​=20%.
  3. Check: 25% of 80 is 20, and 20% of 100 is 20.
Answer: Increase 25%, decrease 20%
Common mistakes
  • Dividing the change by the new value: the rise from 80 to 100 is not 10020​ but 8020​. Rule: divide by the value before the change.
  • Turning 20% into 0.02 instead of 0.2: divide by 100 by moving the point two places, so 20 becomes 0.20.
  • Treating the percent itself as the answer: 20% of 150 is not 20, and not 150−20.
  • Thinking a rise of some percent followed by a fall of the same percent returns the number: the base changes between the steps.
Summary
  • A percent is a fraction over 100.
  • Part of a whole: multiply the percent (as a fraction) by the whole.
  • Percent of a part: part ÷ whole × 100.
  • A percent change is always on the original value.

Discounts and Successive Percent Changes

What you will learn
  • Find the price after a discount
  • Turn increases and decreases into multipliers
  • Find the effect of two changes in a row
  • See why successive percents are not added

Discount

A discount is a part of the original price. A 25% discount on a shirt that costs 200 SAR is 10025​×200=50 SAR, so the price becomes 150 SAR.

Faster: after a 25% discount 75% of the price remains, so the new price is 200×0.75=150.

The multiplier: turning a change into a product

Turn each change into a multiplier: a 20% rise has multiplier 1.2, a 20% fall has 0.8, and a 25% discount has 0.75.

New price = price before the change × multiplier.

Two changes in a row

When a price changes twice, each change is calculated on the price just before it, not on the original. So we do not add the two percents; we multiply the two multipliers.

A 20% rise then a 20% fall gives 1.2×0.8=0.96, a fall of 4%, not zero.

Two discounts in a row

A 20% discount then a 10% discount is not a 30% discount: 200 becomes 160 and then 144, and the total discount is 20056​=28%.

Laws and rules

Increase multipliermultiplier=1+100percent​A 20% rise has multiplier 1.2.
Decrease and discount multipliermultiplier=1−100percent​A 25% discount has multiplier 0.75.
Two successive changesfinal=original×first multiplier×second multiplierThen total percent change = (final − original) ÷ original.

Worked examples

A shirt costs 200 SAR and has a 25% discount. What is its price after the discount?
Show the solution
  1. Discount =10025​×200=50 SAR.
  2. Price after the discount =200−50=150 SAR.
  3. Check: 200×0.75=150.
Answer: 150 SAR
An item costs 200 SAR. It is reduced by 25%, then the new price is increased by 10%. What is the final price?
Show the solution
  1. After the reduction: 200×0.75=150 SAR.
  2. The rise is calculated on 150: 150×1.1=165 SAR.
  3. Check: 150+10% of it =150+15=165.
  4. Total change: 200165−200​=−17.5%, a fall of 17.5%, not 15%.
Answer: 165 SAR
A price of 100 SAR rises 20%, then the new price falls 20%. What is the final price and the total change?
Show the solution
  1. After the rise: 100×1.2=120 SAR.
  2. The 20% fall is calculated on 120: 120×0.8=96 SAR.
  3. Total change =96−100=−4 SAR, a fall of 4%.
  4. Check with multipliers: 1.2×0.8=0.96.
Answer: 96 SAR, a total fall of 4%
Common mistakes
  • Adding the percents: «+20% then −20% = zero» is wrong because the second percent is taken on a larger price. Multiply the multipliers.
  • Calculating the second change on the original price: a 10% rise after the reduction is taken on 150, not on 200.
  • Adding two discount percents: 20% then 10% is 28%, not 30%.
  • Forgetting to subtract the discount: 25% of 200 gives the discount (50), not the new price.
Summary
  • Price after a discount = price × (1 − discount rate).
  • Turn each change into a multiplier such as 1.2 or 0.8.
  • Two successive changes are multiplied, not added.
  • A rise then a fall of the same percent leaves a net fall.

Reverse Percentages and Finding the Whole

What you will learn
  • Find the whole when you know a percent of it
  • Find the original price from the price after a rise or discount
  • Avoid the mistake of subtracting directly

Finding the whole from the part

If «20% of a number is 30», the part and the percent are known and the whole is not. Divide the part by the percent: 30÷0.2=150.

Step-by-step alternative: 20% = 30, so 10% = 15, so 100% = 150.

Reversing a rise or a discount

When the price after a change is given and the original is asked, the new price is a known percent of the original: after a 20% rise it is 120% of the original, and after a 25% discount it is 75%.

So: original = new ÷ multiplier. Do not take the percent of the new price.

Why not just subtract?

The rise was 20% of the original (the smaller number), so it is less than 20% of 180. Subtracting 36 from 180 gives 144, but 144×1.2=172.8, not 180, so that answer is wrong.

Laws and rules

Whole from the partwhole=percentpart​Write the percent as a decimal: 20% becomes 0.2.
Original valueoriginal=multipliernew​The multiplier is 1.2 for a 20% rise and 0.75 for a 25% discount.

Worked examples

20% of a number is 30. What is the number?
Show the solution
  1. Part 30, percent 20%: whole =30÷0.2=150.
  2. Or: 10%=30÷2=15, then 100%=15×10=150.
  3. Check: 20% of 150 is 30.
Answer: 150
After a 20% increase, the price of an item is 180 SAR. What was the original price?
Show the solution
  1. The new price is 120% of the original, multiplier 1.2.
  2. Original =180÷1.2=150 SAR.
  3. Check: 150×1.2=180.
Answer: 150 SAR
After a 25% discount, a device costs 150 SAR. What was its price before the discount?
Show the solution
  1. The price after the discount is 75% of the original, multiplier 0.75.
  2. Original =150÷0.75=200 SAR.
  3. Check: 25% of 200 is 50, and 200−50=150.
Answer: 200 SAR
Common mistakes
  • Taking 20% of 180 and subtracting it: the percent applies to the unknown original, so divide by 1.2.
  • Dividing by 20 instead of 0.2 when finding the whole: turn the percent into a decimal first.
  • Mixing up rise and discount: after a discount you divide by a multiplier below 1 (the result grows); after a rise, by a multiplier above 1.
Summary
  • Whole = part ÷ percent (as a decimal).
  • Original = new ÷ multiplier.
  • Never subtract a percent of the new price.
  • Always check by multiplying by the multiplier.

Rates and Speed

What you will learn
  • Find a unit rate
  • Use the speed, distance and time formula
  • Make units match before calculating
  • Find the average speed of a two-stage trip

Rate

A rate is a ratio between two quantities of different kinds, such as litres per minute. A unit rate is the rate for 1 of the second quantity.

A pump that fills 120 litres in 8 minutes has a rate of 120÷8=15 litres per minute, and from it we find any other amount by multiplying or dividing.

A unit rate helps you compare: a 6 kg pack for 30 SAR costs 5 SAR per kilogram, while a 4 kg pack for 24 SAR costs 6 SAR per kilogram, so the first is the better buy.

Speed

Speed is a rate: how far something travels in one unit of time. One formula gives three forms: speed, distance and time.

A constant speed means distance and time are in direct proportion, so the distance-time graph is a straight line whose slope is the speed.

Make the units match

Two and a half hours is 2.5 hours, and 45 minutes is 0.75 hours. Do not divide kilometres by minutes if the speed is asked in km/h.

To convert km/h to m/s, divide by 3.6; to go back, multiply by 3.6. So 36km/h is 10m/s.

Average speed

Average speed is the total distance divided by the total time, not the average of the two speeds. Someone who covers two equal distances at different speeds spends more time at the lower speed.

Laws and rules

Speedspeed=timedistance​Distance in km and time in hours give speed in km/h.
Distancedistance=speed×timeUsed when speed and time are known.
Timetime=speeddistance​Comes out in hours if the speed is in km/h.
Average speedaverage speed=total timetotal distance​Usually different from the average of the two numbers.

Worked examples

A car covers 150 km in two and a half hours at a constant speed. What is its speed?
Show the solution
  1. Time =2.5 hours and distance =150 km.
  2. Speed =2.5150​=60km/h.
  3. Check: 60×2.5=150.
Answer: 60km/h
A pump fills 120 litres in 8 minutes. How many minutes does it need to fill 300 litres at the same rate?
Show the solution
  1. Pump rate =120÷8=15 litres per minute.
  2. Time =300÷15=20 minutes.
  3. Check: 15×20=300.
Answer: 20 minutes
A car goes 120 km at 60 km/h and comes back the same 120 km at 40 km/h. What is its average speed for the whole trip?
Show the solution
  1. Time going =120÷60=2 hours, time back =120÷40=3 hours.
  2. Total distance =240 km, total time =5 hours.
  3. Average =240÷5=48km/h.
  4. Note: the average of the two numbers, 50, is not the answer.
Answer: 48km/h
Common mistakes
  • Using minutes with km/h: convert time to hours first (45 minutes = 0.75 hours).
  • Reading «two and a half hours» as 2.30: half an hour is 0.5, so the time is 2.5.
  • Averaging the two speeds: the correct average is total distance ÷ total time.
  • Flipping the formula: speed = distance ÷ time, never time ÷ distance.
Summary
  • Rate = one quantity ÷ another, per one unit.
  • Speed = distance ÷ time.
  • Make the units match before calculating.
  • Average speed = total distance ÷ total time.

Linear Models

★ Key
What you will learn
  • Read a linear model and explain its parts
  • Find the output for a given input
  • Find the input for a given output
  • Write the model from two data points

What is a linear model?

A linear model describes a situation where the output changes by a constant amount for every new unit, like a taxi fare: a fixed fee plus a price per kilometre. Its form is y=mx+b.

Here x is the input (kilometres), y is the output (the fare), m is the rate of change (the slope), meaning what is added per unit, and b is the starting value, the fixed fee at x=0.

Using the model

To find the output, substitute x and calculate. To find the input from a known output, substitute y and solve a two-step equation: subtract the constant, then divide by the rate.

A problem may name the variables with other letters such as C for cost and n for the number, but the idea is the same: constant + rate × number.

Writing the model from two points

If you are given the output at two inputs, the rate is m=change in xchange in y​, then substitute one point to find b.

The graph

The graph is a straight line: it crosses the vertical axis at b and its slope is m. The larger m is, the steeper the line.

Laws and rules

Linear modely=mx+bx is the input, y the output, m the rate of change per unit, b the starting value.
Rate from two pointsm=x2​−x1​y2​−y1​​The points are (x1​,y1​) and (x2​,y2​).
Finding the inputx=my−b​Subtract the starting value from the output, then divide by the rate.

Worked examples

A taxi charges a 5 SAR fixed fee plus 2 SAR per kilometre. What is the fare for a 12 km trip?
Show the solution
  1. Model: y=2x+5.
  2. Substitute x=12: y=2×12+5=24+5=29.
  3. Check: 24 SAR for the kilometres plus the 5 SAR fee.
Answer: 29 SAR
A club charges a 20 SAR joining fee plus 5 SAR per training hour. How many hours did someone train who paid 80 SAR?
Show the solution
  1. Model: 80=5x+20.
  2. Subtract the fixed fee: 5x=80−20=60.
  3. Divide by the rate: x=60÷5=12.
  4. Check: 5×12+20=80.
Answer: 12 hours
A plant is 18 cm tall after 2 weeks and 27 cm after 5 weeks, growing at a constant rate. Write the model, then find its height after 6 weeks.
Show the solution
  1. Rate m=5−227−18​=39​=3 cm per week.
  2. Substitute the point (2,18): 18=3×2+b, so b=12.
  3. Model: y=3x+12.
  4. After 6 weeks: y=3×6+12=30 cm.
  5. Check the other point: 3×5+12=27.
Answer: y=3x+12, and the height after 6 weeks is 30 cm
Common mistakes
  • Multiplying the fixed fee by the input: the constant is added once only (5 + 2 × 12, not 7 × 12).
  • Forgetting the fixed fee when finding the input: subtract it from the output before dividing by the rate.
  • Dividing the whole output by the rate: 80 ÷ 5 does not give the hours because 20 of the 80 is the fixed fee.
  • Mixing the rate with the starting value: the rate is what is added per unit, the starting value is the amount at zero.
Summary
  • Linear model: output = rate × input + constant.
  • To find the output, substitute the input.
  • To find the input: subtract the constant, then divide by the rate.
  • Rate from two points = change in output ÷ change in input.
