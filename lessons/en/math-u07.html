Parabolas

★ Key
What you will learn
  • Define a parabola by its focus and directrix
  • Read the focus, directrix and opening direction from its equation
  • Handle shifted parabolas and write an equation from given data

What is a parabola?

A parabola is the set of all points whose distance from a fixed point, the focus, equals their distance from a fixed line, the directrix.

The midpoint of the perpendicular segment from the focus to the directrix is the vertex, and the line through the focus perpendicular to the directrix is the axis.

The two standard equations (vertex at the origin)

Let p be the distance from the vertex to the focus. The variable that is not squared tells you the direction of the axis.

  • y2=4px: the axis is the x-axis, focus (p,0), directrix x=−p; it opens right if p is positive and left if p is negative.
  • x2=4py: the axis is the y-axis, focus (0,p), directrix y=−p; it opens up if p is positive and down if p is negative.

Why divide by 4?

The coefficient multiplying the non-squared variable equals 4p, not p, so divide it by 4 to get p.

Take a point (x,y) on the parabola with focus (p,0) and directrix x=−p. Its distance to the focus equals its distance to the directrix: (x−p)2+y2​=x+p. Squaring both sides leaves y2=4px, which is where the 4 comes from.

The focus and directrix lie on opposite sides of the vertex at the same distance from it, so the directrix is always on the side opposite to the opening.

Width at the focus

At x=p in y2=4px we get y2=4p2, so y=±2p. The points (p,2p) and (p,−2p) lie on the parabola, and the chord through the focus perpendicular to the axis has length 4p (in absolute value).

These two points help you sketch the curve quickly: the vertex and the two points beside the focus.

Shifted parabolas

If the vertex is (h,k), subtract h from x and k from y. Be careful: the sign inside the brackets is the opposite of the vertex coordinate.

Example: in (x−3)2=8(y+1) the vertex is (3,−1) and 4p=8, so p=2. It opens up, so the focus is (3,1) and the directrix is y=−3.

  • (y−k)2=4p(x−h): focus (h+p,k) and directrix x=h−p.
  • (x−h)2=4p(y−k): focus (h,k+p) and directrix y=k−p.

Laws and rules

Opens horizontally (vertex at origin)y2=4pxFocus (p,0) and directrix x=−p; p is the distance from the vertex to the focus, in length units.
Opens vertically (vertex at origin)x2=4pyFocus (0,p) and directrix y=−p.
Shifted parabola(x−h)2=4p(y−k)Vertex (h,k), focus (h,k+p) and directrix y=k−p.

Worked examples

Find the focus and directrix of the parabola y2=12x.
Show the solution
  1. The square is on y, so compare with y2=4px.
  2. 4p=12; divide by 4 to get p=3.
  3. p is positive, so it opens right and the focus is (p,0)=(3,0).
  4. The directrix is x=−p, that is x=−3.
  5. Check: the vertex (0,0) is halfway between (3,0) and the line x=−3.
Answer: Focus (3,0) and directrix x=−3.
A parabola has vertex (1,2) and focus (1,5). Find its equation and directrix.
Show the solution
  1. The vertex and focus have the same x-coordinate 1, so the axis is vertical and the form is (x−h)2=4p(y−k).
  2. p=5−2=3, positive because the focus is above the vertex.
  3. 4p=12 and the vertex is (h,k)=(1,2), so the equation is (x−1)2=12(y−2).
  4. The directrix is y=k−p=2−3=−1.
  5. Check: k+p=5 matches the given focus.
Answer: Equation (x−1)2=12(y−2) and directrix y=−1.
A parabola has its vertex at the origin, the y-axis as its axis, and passes through (4,8). Find its focus.
Show the solution
  1. The axis is vertical and the vertex is the origin, so the equation is x2=4py.
  2. The point lies on the curve, so substitute x=4 and y=8: 16=4p×8=32p.
  3. p=3216​=21​.
  4. The focus is (0,p)=(0,21​).
  5. Check: the equation is x2=2y, and at (4,8) we get 16=16.
Answer: The focus is (0,21​).
Common mistakes
  • Taking the coefficient of x as p directly. In y2=8x the coefficient 8 is 4p, so p=2, not 8.
  • Putting the focus on the wrong axis. Look at the variable that is not squared: y2=…x has its focus on the x-axis, and x2=…y on the y-axis.
  • Reading the vertex with the same sign as the brackets. (y+1) means k=−1, so flip the sign.
  • Placing the directrix on the same side as the opening. It is always on the opposite side: for an upward opening, y=k−p.
Summary
  • A parabola is the set of points equally far from the focus and the directrix.
  • In y2=4px the focus is (p,0) and the directrix is x=−p.
  • The coefficient equals 4p, so always divide it by 4.
  • For a shifted parabola read the vertex with the opposite sign of the brackets; the directrix is on the side opposite the opening.

Ellipses and Circles

★ Key
What you will learn
  • Read the axes lengths and foci from the equation of an ellipse
  • Decide the direction of the major axis from the denominators
  • Write the equation of a circle and find its centre and radius by completing the square

The ellipse

An ellipse is the set of points whose distances to two fixed points, the foci, add up to a constant.

The line through the foci is the major axis, its midpoint is the centre, and the minor axis is perpendicular to it at the centre.

The standard equation

With the centre at the origin and the right side equal to 1, let a be half the major axis, b half the minor axis, and c the distance from each focus to the centre. The larger denominator is always a2.

  • a2x2​+b2y2​=1 with a>b: the major axis is horizontal, vertices (±a,0) and foci (±c,0).
  • b2x2​+a2y2​=1: the major axis is vertical and the foci are (0,±c).
  • The major axis has length 2a and the minor axis has length 2b.

Where are the foci?

The foci are always on the major axis, that is, on the axis of the variable that has the larger denominator. From an end of the minor axis to each focus the distance is a, so a right triangle with sides b and c and hypotenuse a appears, giving c2=a2−b2.

Example: denominators 25 and 9 under x2 and y2 give a=5, a major axis of length 10, and c2=16, so c=4. In 16x2​+25y2​=1 the larger denominator is under y, so the major axis is vertical and the foci are (0,±3).

The circle

A circle is the set of points at a fixed distance, the radius r, from the centre (h,k); its equation is (x−h)2+(y−k)2=r2, and with the centre at the origin it is x2+y2=r2. For example, the circle with centre (2,−3) and radius 4 is (x−2)2+(y+3)2=16.

If the equation is expanded, complete the square for each variable: add to both sides the square of half the coefficient of the linear term. The right side must come out positive for the radius to be real.

Shifted ellipses and eccentricity

If the centre is (h,k), replace x by (x−h) and y by (y−k); the foci move with the centre. For example 25(x−1)2​+9(y+2)2​=1 has centre (1,−2) and c=4, so its foci are (5,−2) and (−3,−2).

The eccentricity is the ratio ac​, always between 0 and 1 because c<a. For 25x2​+9y2​=1 it equals 54​; the closer it is to 0, the closer the ellipse is to a circle.

Worked examples

Find the foci of the ellipse 9x2+16y2=144 and the length of its major axis.
Show the solution
  1. The right side is not 1, so divide both sides by 144: 1449x2​+14416y2​=1.
  2. Simplify: 16x2​+9y2​=1.
  3. The larger denominator 16 is under x, so the major axis is horizontal, a2=16 so a=4, and b2=9.
  4. c2=16−9=7, so c=7​.
  5. The foci are (±7​,0) and the major axis has length 2a=8.
Answer: Foci (±7​,0) and major axis length 8.
An ellipse has its centre at the origin, foci (±3,0) and a major axis of length 10. Write its equation.
Show the solution
  1. The foci are on the x-axis, so the major axis is horizontal and the form is a2x2​+b2y2​=1.
  2. 2a=10 so a=5, and c=3 from the coordinates of the foci.
  3. b2=a2−c2=25−9=16.
  4. The equation is 25x2​+16y2​=1.
  5. Check: 25−16=9, so c=3 as given.
Answer: 25x2​+16y2​=1
Find the centre and radius of the circle x2+y2−6x+8y=0.
Show the solution
  1. Group the terms of each variable: (x2−6x)+(y2+8y)=0.
  2. Half the coefficient of x is −3, its square is 9; half the coefficient of y is 4, its square is 16; add 9 and 16 to both sides.
  3. (x−3)2+(y+4)2=25.
  4. The centre is (3,−4), because the sign inside the brackets is opposite to the coordinate, and the radius is 25​=5.
  5. Check: the origin lies on the circle because 32+42=25 is the square of the radius.
Answer: Centre (3,−4) and radius 5.
Common mistakes
  • Putting the foci on the minor axis or ignoring the axis direction. Find the larger denominator; the foci are on the axis of its variable.
  • Computing c by adding, as for a hyperbola. For an ellipse c2=a2−b2, by subtraction.
  • Reading the denominators before making the right side 1, or treating a as the axis length. In 9x2+16y2=144 the denominators are 16 and 9, not 9 and 16, and the major axis length is 2a.
  • For a circle, reading the centre with the same sign as the brackets. (y+4) means the y-coordinate of the centre is −4.
Summary
  • In an ellipse the larger denominator is a2, and its variable shows where the major axis lies.
  • The major axis has length 2a and the minor axis 2b.
  • The foci are on the major axis, with c2=a2−b2.
  • Make the right side 1 before reading anything.
  • Circle: (x−h)2+(y−k)2=r2; complete the square if it is expanded.

Hyperbolas

What you will learn
  • Recognise the equation of a hyperbola and the direction of its branches
  • Find the vertices, foci and asymptotes
  • Write its equation from the vertices and foci

What is a hyperbola?

A hyperbola is the set of points whose distances to two fixed points, the foci, differ by a constant.

It has two separate branches, and its centre is the midpoint of the segment joining the foci.

The standard equation

Its equation differs from the ellipse by the minus sign between the two terms. The positive term decides the direction of the branches, and its denominator is always a2, even if it is the smaller one.

  • a2x2​−b2y2​=1: the branches open right and left, vertices (±a,0), foci (±c,0).
  • a2y2​−b2x2​=1: the branches open up and down, vertices (0,±a), foci (0,±c).
  • The lengths are related by addition: c2=a2+b2.

The asymptotes

These are two lines that the branches approach but never touch. Draw the rectangle with half-sides a and b around the centre; its extended diagonals are the asymptotes, and the distance from the centre to a corner equals c.

A rule that works for both forms: the slope of the asymptotes is the square root of the y denominator divided by the square root of the x denominator. For 16y2​−9x2​=1 the slope is 34​ and the lines are y=±34​x.

The axes, eccentricity and shifted hyperbolas

The transverse axis is the segment joining the vertices, of length 2a, and the conjugate axis has length 2b. The eccentricity ac​ is always greater than 1 because c>a; in the figure above it equals 45​.

If the centre is (h,k), replace x by (x−h) and y by (y−k) and everything moves with the centre. For example 16(x−2)2​−9(y+1)2​=1 has centre (2,−1) and vertices (6,−1) and (−2,−1).

Laws and rules

Hyperbola with horizontal branchesa2x2​−b2y2​=1Vertices (±a,0) and foci (±c,0); a, b, c are lengths.
Distance from a focus to the centrec2=a2+b2Add for a hyperbola; it does not matter whether a is larger than b.
Asymptotesy=±ab​xFor horizontal branches; for vertical branches use y=±ba​x.

Worked examples

What does 16x2​−9y2​=1 represent? Find its vertices, foci and asymptotes.
Show the solution
  1. There is a minus sign between the terms and the right side is 1, so it is a hyperbola.
  2. The positive term is x2, so the branches open right and left; a2=16 so a=4, and b2=9 so b=3.
  3. The vertices are (±4,0).
  4. c2=16+9=25 so c=5 and the foci are (±5,0).
  5. The slope is ab​=43​, so the asymptotes are y=±43​x.
Answer: A hyperbola: vertices (±4,0), foci (±5,0), asymptotes y=±43​x.
Find the foci and the asymptotes of the hyperbola 16y2​−9x2​=1.
Show the solution
  1. The positive term is y2, so the branches open up and down.
  2. The denominator of y2 is 16 so a=4, and the denominator of x2 is 9 so b=3.
  3. Slope = square root of the y denominator over the square root of the x denominator =34​, so the lines are y=±34​x.
  4. c2=16+9=25 so c=5 and the foci are (0,±5).
Answer: Foci (0,±5) and asymptotes y=±34​x.
A hyperbola has its centre at the origin, vertices (±4,0) and foci (±5,0). Write its equation.
Show the solution
  1. The vertices are on the x-axis, so the form is a2x2​−b2y2​=1.
  2. a=4 from the vertex and c=5 from the focus.
  3. b2=c2−a2=25−16=9.
  4. The equation is 16x2​−9y2​=1.
  5. Check: 16+9=25=c2.
Answer: 16x2​−9y2​=1
Common mistakes
  • Treating the larger denominator as a2, as in the ellipse. In a hyperbola a2 is the denominator of the positive term even if it is smaller: in 9x2​−16y2​=1 we have a=3, b=4 and the foci are (±5,0).
  • Computing c by subtraction. For a hyperbola c2=a2+b2; only the ellipse uses subtraction.
  • Inverting the slope of the asymptotes for the vertical form. Use the rule: square root of the y denominator over the square root of the x denominator in both cases.
Summary
  • A minus sign between x2 and y2 means a hyperbola.
  • The positive term gives the direction of the branches, and its denominator is a2.
  • c2=a2+b2, and the foci lie on the axis of the branches.
  • Asymptote slope = square root of the y denominator ÷ square root of the x denominator.

Identifying Conic Sections

What you will learn
  • Tell a circle, ellipse, parabola and hyperbola apart from the form of the equation
  • Convert the equation to standard form to confirm

The conic sections

A circle, ellipse, parabola and hyperbola all come from cutting a cone with a plane at different slopes, which is why they are called conic sections.

They are all second-degree equations in x and y, and we identify the type by looking at the squared terms.

Questions that decide the type

Move all terms to one side (or make the right side 1), then ask:

  • Is only one variable squared, with a first-degree term in the other? Then it is a parabola, and so is y=x2−4 because only one variable is squared.
  • Do x2 and y2 appear with opposite signs? Then it is a hyperbola.
  • Do they have the same sign? If the coefficients are equal it is a circle; if they differ it is an ellipse.

Confirm with the standard form

After deciding, convert to standard form to be sure. For example, in 4x2+9y2=36 both squares are positive with different coefficients, and dividing by 36 gives 9x2​+4y2​=1, an ellipse.

Laws and rules

Parabolay2=4pxOnly one variable is squared.
Ellipsea2x2​+b2y2​=1Two squares with the same sign and different coefficients.
Circlex2+y2=r2Two squares with the same sign and equal coefficients.
Hyperbolaa2x2​−b2y2​=1Two squares with opposite signs.

Worked examples

What does the equation 4x2+9y2=36 represent?
Show the solution
  1. Both x2 and y2 appear with positive signs and different coefficients 4 and 9.
  2. Divide both sides by 36: 9x2​+4y2​=1.
  3. This is the form of an ellipse, with a horizontal major axis because a=3 and b=2.
Answer: An ellipse.
What does the equation y2−4x+2y=0 represent?
Show the solution
  1. Only y2 appears, there is no x2, and the other term is linear in x, so we expect a parabola.
  2. Complete the square for y: y2+2y=4x, then add 1 to both sides: (y+1)2=4x+1.
  3. Factor out 4: (y+1)2=4(x+41​), which has the form (y−k)2=4p(x−h) with p=1.
  4. Its vertex is (−41​,−1) and it opens to the right.
Answer: A parabola.
What does the equation 9x2−4y2=36 represent?
Show the solution
  1. The squares have opposite signs, so we expect a hyperbola.
  2. Divide both sides by 36: 4x2​−9y2​=1.
  3. The branches open right and left, with a=2, b=3 and c=13​ because 4+9=13.
Answer: A hyperbola.
Common mistakes
  • Deciding before moving the terms. The equation x2=4−y2 seems to have no minus sign, but moving the term gives x2+y2=4, a circle.
  • Thinking any equation with a linear term is a parabola. x2+y2−6x=0 has two squares with equal coefficients, so it is a circle.
  • Confusing the circle with the ellipse. The only difference is that the coefficients of x2 and y2 are equal in a circle.
  • After completing the square for a circle or ellipse, ignoring the right side. If it comes out zero or negative there is no real curve, so check its sign before reading the radius.
Summary
  • Only one square: a parabola.
  • Two squares with opposite signs: a hyperbola.
  • Two squares with the same sign: a circle if the coefficients are equal, otherwise an ellipse.
  • Move terms or divide to reach standard form before deciding.

Vectors in the Plane

★ Key
What you will learn
  • Tell a vector quantity from a scalar quantity
  • Find the components, length, direction and unit vector of a vector
  • Add and subtract vectors and recognise parallel vectors

Scalar and vector quantities

A scalar quantity has magnitude only, such as mass, time, distance, temperature and energy. A vector quantity has both magnitude and direction, such as displacement, velocity, force and acceleration.

We draw a vector as an arrow: its length is the magnitude and the arrowhead shows the direction. Equal vectors have the same length and direction, wherever they are placed.

The resultant

The resultant of two vectors is the single vector with the same effect as both: place the tail of the second at the head of the first, and the resultant runs from the tail of the first to the head of the second. In the same direction the lengths add; in opposite directions subtract the smaller from the larger.

If the vectors are perpendicular the resultant is the hypotenuse of a right triangle: perpendicular vectors of lengths 6 and 8 have a resultant of 36+64​=10, not 14.

A vector by components

In the plane we write a vector with two components (x,y): the horizontal change, then the vertical change. The vector from point A(x1​,y1​) to point B(x2​,y2​) is the end minus the start: (x2​−x1​,y2​−y1​).

Length, direction and unit vector

The length of (x,y) comes from Pythagoras: x2+y2​. Its direction angle θ with the x-axis satisfies tanθ=xy​; for example, for (3​,1) we get tanθ=3​1​, so θ=30∘.

A unit vector has length 1 and is found by dividing the vector by its length. For (3,4) the length is 5, so the unit vector is (53​,54​).

The tangent alone does not fix the quadrant, so sketch the vector: (−1,1) has direction angle 135∘, not −45∘. Conversely, a vector of known length and direction angle θ has components length ×cosθ and length ×sinθ; a vector of length 10 at 30∘ has components (53​,5).

Operations and parallel vectors

  • Addition and subtraction: add or subtract the matching components.
  • Multiplying by a number: multiply each component by it; the length is multiplied by the absolute value of the number, and the direction reverses if the number is negative.
  • Parallel: (x1​,y1​) and (x2​,y2​) are parallel if their components are proportional, that is x1​y2​=y1​x2​.

Worked examples

Find the vector from A(2,−1) to B(5,3), its length, and the unit vector in its direction.
Show the solution
  1. End minus start: (5−2,3−(−1))=(3,4).
  2. Length: 32+42​=25​=5.
  3. Unit vector: divide each component by 5: (53​,54​).
  4. Check: its length is 259​+2516​​=1.

If a=(2,−1) and b=(−3,5), find 3a+b and the length of a+b.
Show the solution
  1. 3a=(6,−3), multiplying each component by 3.
  2. 3a+b=(6+(−3),−3+5)=(3,2).
  3. a+b=(2+(−3),−1+5)=(−1,4).
  4. Its length is (−1)2+42​=1+16​=17​.

Find the value of n that makes the vectors (2,n) and (n,8) parallel.
Show the solution
  1. The parallel condition x1​y2​=y1​x2​ gives 2×8=n×n.
  2. n2=16.
  3. n=4 or n=−4.
  4. Check: for (2,4) and (4,8) the second is twice the first; for (2,−4) and (−4,8) the second is −2 times the first.
Answer: n=4 or n=−4.
Common mistakes
  • Confusing distance with displacement. Distance is a scalar and always positive; displacement is a vector depending on the start, the end and the direction.
  • Subtracting the wrong way round for the vector between two points. The rule is end minus start, and watch the negative: 3−(−1)=4.
  • Adding the two lengths to get the length of the resultant. Addition is done by components; lengths add only in the same direction, so perpendicular 6 and 8 give 10, not 14.
  • Dropping the negative solution in the parallel condition: n2=16 has two solutions.
Summary
  • A vector quantity has magnitude and direction; a scalar has magnitude only.
  • The vector between two points is end minus start, and its length is x2+y2​.
  • Unit vector = vector ÷ its length.
  • Addition, subtraction and scaling are done by components.
  • Parallel: x1​y2​=y1​x2​.

The Dot Product

What you will learn
  • Compute the dot product from components and from lengths and angle
  • Use it to find the angle between two vectors
  • Use it to test for perpendicular vectors

The definition by components

The dot product of two vectors is a number, not a vector. Multiply the matching components and add: a⋅b=x1​x2​+y1​y2​.

The geometric form and the angle

If θ is the angle between the vectors when their tails meet, the dot product equals the product of the two lengths times cosθ. For example, with lengths 4 and 5 and an angle of 60∘: 4×5×21​=10.

Comparing the two forms gives the angle: divide the dot product by the product of the lengths to get cosθ.

Perpendicular vectors

If the dot product is zero and neither vector is zero, then cosθ=0, so θ=90∘ and the vectors are perpendicular; the converse is also true.

A positive result means an acute angle, and a negative result means an obtuse angle.

Useful properties and the projection

The dot product is commutative: a⋅b=b⋅a. A vector dotted with itself gives the square of its length: a⋅a=∣a∣2. For parallel vectors cosθ=±1, so the dot product equals ±∣a∣∣b∣.

The length of the projection of a on b (the perpendicular shadow of a on the line of b) is ∣b∣a⋅b​. In the figure above it is 22​=1, the x-coordinate of a.

Worked examples

If a=(3,2) and b=(−1,4), what is a⋅b?
Show the solution
  1. Multiply the x-components: 3×(−1)=−3.
  2. Multiply the y-components: 2×4=8.
  3. Add: −3+8=5.
  4. The result is a number, not a vector, and it is positive, so the angle between the vectors is acute.
Answer: a⋅b=5.
Find the value of n that makes the vectors (n,4) and (6,−3) perpendicular.
Show the solution
  1. Perpendicular means the dot product is zero.
  2. 6n+4×(−3)=6n−12=0.
  3. 6n=12 so n=2.
  4. Check: (2,4)⋅(6,−3)=12−12=0.
Answer: n=2.
Find the angle between a=(1,3​) and b=(2,0).
Show the solution
  1. Dot product: 1×2+3​×0=2.
  2. Lengths: ∣a∣=1+3​=2 and ∣b∣=4+0​=2.
  3. cosθ=2×22​=21​.
  4. θ=60∘ because cos60∘=21​.
  5. Check: the tangent of the direction angle of a is 3​, so it is 60∘, and b lies along the x-axis.
Answer: The angle is 60∘.
Common mistakes
  • Writing the dot product as a vector such as (−3,8). The two products are added, giving one number.
  • Applying the perpendicular test to the sum or difference instead of the dot product. Perpendicular means a⋅b=0 only.
  • Forgetting to divide by the product of the lengths when finding the angle, and setting cosθ equal to the dot product alone.
Summary
  • Dot product by components: x1​x2​+y1​y2​, and the result is a number.
  • It equals the product of the lengths times the cosine of the angle between them.
  • Cosine of the angle = dot product ÷ product of the lengths.
  • Perpendicular means the dot product is zero.

Vectors in Space

What you will learn
  • Find the length of a vector in space
  • Apply the dot product with three components and test for perpendicular vectors
  • Compute the cross product and check the result

Three components

We add a third axis z perpendicular to the xy-plane, so a vector has three components (x,y,z). What we learned in the plane carries over by adding the third component.

  • Length: x2+y2+z2​ (Pythagoras twice). For example, for (2,3,6): 4+9+36=49 and the length is 7.
  • Dot product: x1​x2​+y1​y2​+z1​z2​, and the vectors are perpendicular when it equals zero.
  • The vector from A(x1​,y1​,z1​) to B(x2​,y2​,z2​) is again end minus start, and the unit vector is the vector divided by its length.

The cross product

Two vectors in space have another product whose result is a vector: the cross product a×b, which is perpendicular to both vectors. Its components follow the cyclic order x→y→z→x:

  • First: y1​z2​−z1​y2​
  • Second: z1​x2​−x1​z2​
  • Third: x1​y2​−y1​x2​

The length of the cross product

The length of a×b is ∣a∣∣b∣sinθ, the area of the parallelogram with the two vectors as sides. For the vectors of the third example the result is (1,−2,6) with length 1+4+36​=41​, so the parallelogram has area 41​ square units.

For parallel vectors sinθ=0, so the cross product is zero. Its direction follows the right-hand rule: curl the fingers of your right hand from a towards b and the thumb points along a×b.

How to check

The cross product is perpendicular to both vectors, so its dot product with either one is zero. Use that to verify.

Note that the order matters: b×a is the opposite of a×b (all signs reversed).

Laws and rules

Length of a vector in spacex2+y2+z2​x, y and z are the components of the vector.
Dot product in spacex1​x2​+y1​y2​+z1​z2​The result is a number, and it is zero for perpendicular vectors.
Cross producta×b=(y1​z2​−z1​y2​,z1​x2​−x1​z2​,x1​y2​−y1​x2​)The result is a vector perpendicular to both a and b.
Length of the cross product∣a×b∣=∣a∣∣b∣sinθθ is the angle between the vectors; the result is the area of the parallelogram with sides a and b, in area units.

Worked examples

What is the length of the vector (2,3,6)?
Show the solution
  1. Add the squares of the components: 22+32+62=4+9+36=49.
  2. The length is 49​=7.
  3. Check: 7×7=49.
Answer: The length is 7.
Find the value of n that makes the vector (n,2,1) perpendicular to the vector (3,−4,2).
Show the solution
  1. Perpendicular means the dot product is zero.
  2. 3n+2×(−4)+1×2=3n−8+2=3n−6.
  3. 3n−6=0 so n=2.
  4. Check: (2,2,1)⋅(3,−4,2)=6−8+2=0.
Answer: n=2.
If a=(2,1,0) and b=(0,3,1), find a×b.
Show the solution
  1. The data: x1​=2, y1​=1, z1​=0, and x2​=0, y2​=3, z2​=1.
  2. First: y1​z2​−z1​y2​=1×1−0×3=1.
  3. Second: z1​x2​−x1​z2​=0×0−2×1=−2.
  4. Third: x1​y2​−y1​x2​=2×3−1×0=6.
  5. The result is (1,−2,6). Check: with (2,1,0) we get 2−2+0=0, and with (0,3,1) we get 0−6+6=0.
Answer: a×b=(1,−2,6).
Common mistakes
  • Forgetting the third component when finding the length or the dot product.
  • Mixing up the two products: the dot product is a number, the cross product is a vector.
  • Reversing the sign of the second component. It is z1​x2​−x1​z2​ in the cyclic order; verify by taking the dot product of the result with one of the original vectors.
  • Assuming a×b=b×a. Swapping the order reverses all the signs.
Summary
  • A vector in space has three components, and its length is the square root of the sum of their squares.
  • The dot product adds three products, and zero means perpendicular.
  • The cross product is a vector perpendicular to both vectors.
  • Check a cross product by its dot product with each original vector: it gives zero.
